# 11-3-2026_dataProject_advCS_mannoia
Link & Description
==================
My file is a REEF Marine Life Survey Geographic Zone Report. Specifically, it contains data from between Jupiter Inlet and Key Biscayne, Florida, from 1993-2026. I got it from the REEF (Reef Environmental Education Foundation) website, and I chose it because I am actually REEF certified to conduct marine life surveys in this region (Tropical Western Atlantic), and my survey from a dive in Palm Beach earlier this year is part of this data!

REEF collects its marine life data through volunteer surveys. Certified surveyors can take a special underwater slate and record species and amount of said species seen on their dive, and then put it into REEF's database. The organization collects data on fish and all other kinds of species, from turtles to octopi. These surveys therefore become a really useful tool for tracking marine life behavior and patterns.

The data is organized into three sections:
1. There is a header of metadata at the top, which contains the total survey count, the date range of these surveys, and the average species reported by Expert vs. Novice surveyors.
2. This section contains location and zone data. It contains dive site codes, the names of these, and breaks down the number of surveys at each of them. It also separates these them by survey type (Species & Abundance vs. Species Only) and diver type (Expert vs. Novice), as well as includes a column for total bottom time (total time spent underwater).
3. Finally, the third and last section contains the bulk of the data: the actual results from these surveys. It contains almost four hundred species ranked by sightings, and includes columns for Rank, Species ID, Species Name, Family, Sighting Frequency, and Density, broken down across all surveyors.

Questions & Hypotheses
======================
Questions:
1. [Percentage] What percentage of all 388 reported species belong to the top 3 most prevalent reef fish families (Damselfish, Grunts, and Wrasses)?
2. [Combinations/Helper] Of all 388 species recorded, what percentage are "commonly encountered" (have an overall Sighting Frequency of > 50% across all surveys)?
3. [Comparison] Does the Grunt family (Haemulidae) or the Wrasse family (Labridae) have a higher total number of recorded species in this zone?
4. [Range] Looking at species ranked 1 through 20 by sighting frequency, how many maintain an overall Density Score of 2.5 or higher, indicating moderate-to-high abundance?

Hypotheses:
1. These top 3 key families will account for over 25% of all distinct species reported in this zone report, since for the most part, I've seen the same kind of reef fish families prevalent across dives.
2. Fewer than 10% of total species will exceed a 50% Sighting Frequency, as reef fish communities are usually dominated by a small core group of common species while most species are rare.
3. The Grunt family will have a higher number of species recorded than the Wrasse family in  waters.
4. At least 10 of the top 20 communities will have a density score of 2.5 or higher, showing that frequent sightings strongly correlate with high individual count densities.
